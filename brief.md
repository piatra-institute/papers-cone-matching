# Brief

## Question

Chen and Venkatesh proved that two pairs of singular spaces of the form $X = \overline{T'U'\backslash G}$ (the nilpotent cone of $3\times 3$ matrices and the hyperdeterminant hypersurface of $(2,2,2)$-tensors, with their duals) are numerically weakly dual. The decisive step is that the inequalities describing integral points of $X$ coincide with the inequalities describing the weights of regular functions on $\check X$. They say this rests on numerical coincidences specific to their groups, and Remark 7.4 of their paper expects the underlying convex reduction to occur only for groups isogenous to their examples. For which split reductive groups with one-dimensional centre does the same recipe produce matching inequalities, and does matching of the real cones imply matching of the lattice-point sets that index the local factors?

## Claim

1. The automorphic inequalities reduce to the simpler ones (the convex-hull phenomenon of Remark 7.4) exactly for root types $A_2$, $B_2$, $G_2$ and $A_1^3$.
2. The two cones coincide exactly when, in addition, the primitive central pairing $d = \langle\theta_G,\nu_G\rangle$ equals $1/(\kappa-1)$; this leaves $(A_2, d=1)$ and $(A_1^3, d=2)$. So the literal reading of Remark 7.4 fails for $B_2$ and $G_2$, while the full matching singles out the published types.
3. Proposition 5.1 of the source needs the condition $\langle\lambda,\check\rho'\rangle\in\mathbb Z$ when $\check\rho'$ is only rational; with it, equal cones give equal supports if and only if the shifted cocharacters of both groups are genuine cocharacters.
4. For type $A_1^3$ with $d=2$, five of 35 labelled global forms pass both integrality tests (the Garrett pair and three copies of the self-dual $\mathrm{SO}_4\times\mathrm{GL}_2$); for $\mathrm{GL}_2\times\mathrm{PGL}_2\times\mathrm{PGL}_2$ one support comparison fails from central degree 3.

## Strongest alternative

The source paper already identifies the matching of integrality inequalities with weights as the mechanism of its proof and expects it to be exceptional; the cone classification could be regarded as a routine elaboration. The paper's position: the classification is short but not stated in the source, it shows that the convex-hull phenomenon alone is not exceptional to the isogeny classes named there, and the integrality condition is a concrete qualification of a stated proposition that changes the answer for isogenous global forms.
